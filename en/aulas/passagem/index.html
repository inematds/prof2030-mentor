Lesson 6 · 20 min

Safe handoff to IT

Approved in testing doesn't mean ready for production. The prototype runs on Patrícia's laptop, with made-up data. To become a company tool, it goes to Rafael in IT, with everything he needs to validate, connect, and maintain it: code, rules, tests, access, owner, cost, and a way to turn it off.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1What changes from prototype to production
  2. 2Access and secrets in the company account
  3. 3LGPD (Brazil's data protection law) applied to the tool
  4. 4The complete handoff package

Goal: build the handoff package for IT with access, documentation, owner, cost, LGPD (Brazil's data protection law), and a shutdown plan.

    A manager who owns the process, a complete package, technical review by IT, production on a company account with a shutdown that takes minutes, and two named owners.

Honest scope: the mentor organizes the handoff; technical security assessment and penetration testing stay with IT or a qualified specialist.

02 · Four handoff safeguards

Click each step.

1. Access and secrets

Every account (agent, database, AI provider, WhatsApp) is in the company's name, with IT as administrator. No key in a text file, in the code, or in the browser.

Each access gets the lowest permission that does the job: read-only where reading is enough. A list of who accesses sensitive data, in writing.

Warning sign"The key is in my personal email" or "the database is on my free account."

2. Data and LGPD (Brazil's data protection law)

The company is the controller of its customers' data. The AI provider and whoever deploys the tool act as processors: they use the data only for the project, under a contract.

Write down where each piece of data goes, including outside the country, on what legal basis, and how long it is kept. The lab's failure F10 is customer conversations going to a foreign API with no contract.

Short documentA lean impact report of about two pages: what data, for what purpose, with whom, for how long, what risks, and what measures.

3. Shutting down and rolling back

Before you switch it on, agree how to shut it down in minutes and go back to doing the process by hand. Test this once, for real.

Start with part of the volume and watch it closely for the first few days. Never switch it on on a Friday afternoon.

At HorizontePatrícia knows how to pause it from the dashboard; Rafael knows how to roll back to the previous version. Both tested it on a Tuesday morning.

4. Owner, cost, and exit

Write down the estimated monthly cost (AI, hosting, messages) and who pays. Write down who owns the process and who owns the technical side.

If the mentor or a vendor leaves, the company receives everything: versioned rules, knowledge base, exported code, access on its own account, documentation, and a shutdown plan.

Carlos's question"If you disappeared tomorrow, could Rafael keep it running?" The package must let you answer yes.

Handoff package for IT
# Handoff package — Quote follow-up · v1.1
Date: <dd/mm> · Process owner: Patrícia Lima · Technical owner: Rafael (IT) · Mentor: <name>

## 1. What it is
Suggests a follow-up for quotes open for 2 business days; Patrícia approves before it is sent.

## 2. Where it lives
Repository: <address, on a company account> · version accepted: v1.1 (<commit>)
Files: spec.md · AGENTS.md (constitution) · bateria.md (15 cases + results) · lacunas.md

## 3. Access needed (IT creates it on a company account)
| System | Minimum permission | Who administers |
|---|---|---|
| Quote export | read | Rafael |
| Tracking spreadsheet | write only in the "follow-up on" column | Rafael |
| AI provider | production key held by IT | Rafael |
| Message sending | only after approval on screen | Rafael |

## 4. Data
Uses: customer company name, contact, purchase history, open quotes.
Does not use: CPF, bank details, whole old conversations.
Where it goes: <AI provider, country, contract/DPA> · Kept for: <period> · Legal basis: <define with the company>

## 5. Tests
Test suite v1.1: 13/15, no critical case failed. Accepted by Patrícia on <date>.
Before production: expand to 30+ real, anonymized cases (IT + Patrícia).

## 6. Operation
Log of every action: <where> · Pause: <how> · Shut down and go back to manual: <steps, tested on dd/mm>
Start: 30% of quotes in week 1, daily monitoring.

## 7. Estimated cost
AI: R$ <x>/month · hosting: R$ <y>/month · messages: R$ <z>/month · who pays: <cost center>

## 8. Owners and review
Process: Patrícia · Technical: Rafael · Backup: Carlos Mendes · 1st review: <date + 30 days>

## 9. Exit without dependence
Everything is on a company account. If the mentor or a vendor leaves: rules, code, access, documentation, and the shutdown plan stay with IT.
IT review checklist (Rafael works through it)
TECHNICAL REVIEW · tool: <name> · reviewer: <IT name> · date: <dd/mm>

[ ] No key, password, or token in the code, in the HTML, or in the Git history   (F1)
[ ] Every table has an access rule: users read only what they may                 (F2)
[ ] Every admin page requires login                                               (F3)
[ ] Dependencies with no known vulnerability (package manager audit)              (F4)
[ ] Critical limits enforced by permission, not just by instruction               (F5, F6)
[ ] Action log and human approval on the defined amounts and actions              (F7)
[ ] No personal data in an open spreadsheet or link                               (F8)
[ ] Integrations with the lowest permission; no deleting or sending without approval (F9)
[ ] AI provider under contract; data destination recorded                         (F10)
[ ] Accounts in the company's name; backup and restore tested
[ ] Shutting down and going back to manual tested once

Decision: [ ] approves  [ ] approves with caveats: <which>  [ ] does not approve: <reason>
This review does not replace a penetration test or legal advice.
Simulation: illustrative example
  1. No field in the package left blank or with < >.
  2. Two named owners: process and technical.
  3. Estimated monthly cost and who pays.
  4. Shutdown tested once, with a date.

Company accountMinimum access, keys with IT, nothing in plain text.
Data with a destinationCompany as controller, provider under contract, destination and retention period noted.
