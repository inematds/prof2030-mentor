Lesson 7 · 18 min

Measure and improve

A tool turned on without a baseline is a bet: no one knows whether it helped. That's lab flaw F11. In this lesson, you and the manager build a dashboard with three indicators, a short weekly review, and a way to improve without breaking what already works.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Three indicators with a denominator
  2. 2The 15-minute weekly review
  3. 3One hypothesis, one change
  4. 4Keep it or roll it back, with evidence

Goal: build the 3-indicator dashboard with a baseline and apply 1 tested improvement, with a decision to keep it or roll back.

  1. 1/6

    Baseline

    The number from before, with the period and the denominator. At Horizonte: 17 of 42 quotes with no reply within 48 hours in one week. Without this, any improvement is just opinion.

  2. 2/6

    3 indicators

    Three numbers, no more. Business result with usage, quality with zero limits violated, and cost per task including the person's time.

  3. 3/6

    Weekly review

    Every week, the manager and the mentor look at the three numbers and the list of exceptions. Usage dropping, edits rising, or a limit violated sets off an alert.

  4. 4/6

    Hypothesis

    One concrete change, with the number it should move and the evidence behind it. Make two changes at once and nobody knows which one worked.

  5. 5/6

    Test and decide

    The new version runs the same test suite. It stays only if it improves the target without making the others worse. Otherwise, it goes back to the previous one, which you saved.

  6. 6/6

    The whole cycle

    Baseline, three indicators, weekly review, one hypothesis at a time, and a decision to keep or roll back using the same test suite.

A target on its own teaches people to cheat: the target is always "improve X without making Y and Z worse."

02 · How to measure without fooling yourself

Click each step.

1. Always the denominator

"We did 30 follow-ups" tells you nothing. "30 of 41 eligible quotes this week got a reply within 48 h" does.

Keep test, pilot, and production separate: a test suite number is not an operations number.

At HorizonteBefore: 25 of 42 (60%) with a reply within 48 h. Week 3 with the tool: 36 of 40 (90%). Fictional lab data.

2. Cost includes the person

Cost per follow-up = AI and hosting cost + the manager's minutes reviewing and handling exceptions. A cheap tool that doubles the review saves nothing.

AI prices fall fast. Example: on 09/22/2026, Anthropic released a model that, according to the company, costs 40% less than the previous one for typical tasks. So review your model choice every quarter by running the test suite.

Simple mathBefore: 6 min per follow-up. Now: 1.5 min of review + R$ of AI. The difference becomes Patrícia's time with customers.

3. Weekly review

Did it improve against the baseline? Did quality and exceptions change? How much human work was needed? What was the full cost? Does anything need a pause or less autonomy? What hypothesis is worth testing? Who does it, and when?

Warning signs: usage drops from one week to the next, the team goes back to the old way, edits to the drafts go up.

RuleWith fewer than 30 observations, note the trend, but don't decide.

4. Improve without regression

Write the hypothesis: if we change this, then this number goes from A to B, because we saw this evidence. Apply only that change in a new version.

Run the test suite from lesson 5. It stays if it improves the target and nothing else gets worse beyond the tolerance; otherwise, roll back. The previous version stays saved in Git.

At HorizonteHypothesis: mentioning the last product purchased cuts editing from 25% to 15%. The test suite passed, and editing dropped to 16% in 3 weeks. It stayed.

03 · Templates: dashboard, hypothesis card, and weekly review

Copy to a spreadsheet or a file in the repository.

3-indicator dashboard — follow-up

| Indicator | Formula (with denominator) | Baseline | Target | Current week |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Usage and result | quotes with a reply within 48 h ÷ eligible quotes | 25/42 = 60% (<date>) | 85% | <x/y> |
| 2. Quality | drafts approved without edits ÷ drafts generated | — (none existed) | 80% | <x/y> |
|    gate | limits violated during the week | — | 0 | <n> |
| 3. Cost per follow-up | (AI cost + hosting) ÷ follow-ups + review minutes | 6 min of a person's time | <= 2 min + R$ <x> | <value> |

Alert: usage fell 20% in the week · editing above 30% · any limit violated -> pause and review.
Hypothesis card (one change at a time)
## H<n> — <short title>
IF        <a single concrete change>
THEN      <indicator> goes from <current> to <expected>
BECAUSE   <evidence: cases, exceptions, manager comments>
WITHOUT WORSENING <watched indicators and tolerance>
TEST      suite v<x> (same cases) + <n> weeks of operation
RESULT    <before -> after, with denominator>
DECISION  [ stays ]  [ rolled back ]  [ wait for more data ]   by: <name>  on: <date>
Manager's weekly review (15 min)
1. Did the result improve compared to the baseline, over the same period?
2. Did quality, violated limits, and exceptions change?
3. How much human work was needed, and where?
4. What was the full cost, including rework?
5. Does any action need a pause or less autonomy?
6. Which hypothesis deserves a test this week?
7. Who does each action, and when do we review?
Simulation: illustrative example
  1. Usage and result: 36/40 = 90% (baseline 60%).
  2. Quality: 31/40 approved without edits = 78%; limits violated: 0.
  3. Cost: 1.5 min of review + AI cost per follow-up.

Fictional lab data.

  • Number without a denominator: "30 follow-ups" doesn't tell you whether things improved.
  • Cost without the person: hides the review time that grew.
  • Target alone: "more messages" teaches you to send too many messages.

04 · Practice: dashboard and one improvement

With the manager, over three weeks.

E1 · Baseline

30 min

Using the cases from lesson 2, write the "before" number for each indicator, with the period and denominator.

Done when: all three indicators have a "before" value, or are marked "none existed" with the reason.

E2 · Dashboard and reviews

15 min per week

Fill in the dashboard and do the weekly review for three weeks.

Done when: three weeks are filled in, and one action is noted in each review.

E3 · One improvement

60 min

Write one hypothesis, apply one change, run the test suite, and decide.

Done when: the hypothesis card is filled in with the result, the decision (stays or rolled back), and the name of who decided.
