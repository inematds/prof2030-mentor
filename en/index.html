AI Mentor: support the builder manager, from prototype to a safe handoff to IT

Managers are already building their own tools with AI. What's missing is someone to guide them with a method: choose what is worth building, prototype, set limits, test, and hand off to IT with a named owner and a cost. In eight lessons you build that method and leave with a 90-day program plan.

Start with lesson 1 See Professional 2030
  • 8short lessons
  • 8templates to copy
  • 1continuous case at Horizonte
  • 90program days

The lessons

One case from start to finish: sales manager Patrícia Lima wants a customer follow-up tool. You guide her through the handoff to Rafael, from IT.

  1. Lesson 1 · 15 minThe builder managerThe manager who knows the process can now build her own tool with AI. That is good, and it is risky. The mentor is there so her idea becomes something useful, tested, and handed off to IT, without turning into one more hidden tool with exposed customer data.
  2. Lesson 2 · 18 minIs it worth building?Today you can build almost anything in an afternoon. That is why "it can be done" is no longer a reason. Before the prototype, the mentor runs the idea through a short filter and, if it survives, helps the manager write it on one page: the process, the result, what the tool can do and what it never does.
  3. Lesson 3 · 20 minPrototype in one dayWith the spec approved, the manager builds the first prototype in a working session with you. They drive the keyboard; you hold the scope, the fictional data, and the checking. By the end of the day there is a tool running on their computer and an honest list of what's missing.
  4. Lesson 4 · 18 minAutonomy with limitsThe prototype works, but it still decides on its own where the spec says nothing: that is how the 10% discount showed up. The agent constitution is a page that says what the agent exists for, what comes first in a conflict, what it never does, and when it calls a person. The manager writes it; the mentor helps tighten it.
  5. Lesson 5 · 20 minTest before you turn it on"It worked" doesn't tell you in how many cases, or which error slipped through. Before any real use, the mentor and the manager build a test suite with the right answer written down before running, score it with criteria you can count, and only then call the user in to run acceptance testing.
  6. Lesson 6 · 20 minSafe handoff to ITApproved in testing doesn't mean ready for production. The prototype runs on Patrícia's laptop, with made-up data. To become a company tool, it goes to Rafael in IT, with everything he needs to validate, connect, and maintain it: code, rules, tests, access, owner, cost, and a way to turn it off.
  7. Lesson 7 · 18 minMeasure and improveA tool turned on without a baseline is a bet: no one knows whether it helped. That's lab flaw F11. In this lesson, you and the manager build a dashboard with three indicators, a short weekly review, and a way to improve without breaking what already works.
  8. Lesson 8 · 20 minThe 90-day program and what changedThe seven lessons become a program: in 90 days, one to three managers take one tool each from the map to the handoff to IT, with fixed meetings and milestones you can check. At the end, the final project at Horizonte and the dashboard of what changed in the market this quarter.

Who it's for

  1. 1People who want to work helping companies adopt AI, guiding managers who build their own tools.
  2. 2Area managers and IT leaders who will organize this movement inside the company.
  3. 3You don't need to code. You need to know the process, ask good questions, and keep the pace.

What you'll be able to do

  1. 1Filter ideas and turn a process into a one-page spec, with permissions.
  2. 2Run a prototyping session and write the agent constitution.
  3. 3Build the test suite, run acceptance testing with the user, and deliver the handoff package to IT.
  4. 4Measure with three indicators and build a 90-day program with meetings and milestones.
